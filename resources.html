FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a job offer to immigrate to Canada?

Not always. Express Entry programs like Federal Skilled Worker can succeed without a job offer if your CRS score is high enough. However, for trades, hospitality and food service roles, the most reliable pathway is a Canadian employer’s LMIA-supported job offer plus a closed work permit. We’ll help you figure out which route fits.

How much does it cost to work with Amicus?

Our fees are quoted up front for the full file — no surprises and no drip pricing. The exact amount depends on your program, family size and document situation, but our pricing is deliberately set to be among the most economical in the GTA. Government fees (IRCC, biometrics, etc.) are separate and we’ll explain those clearly.

How long does the process take?

It varies widely. A straightforward LMIA-supported work permit might be ready in 3–6 months from start to landing. PR pathways usually take longer — anywhere from 6 to 24 months depending on the program and your country of residence. We give you realistic timelines on the first call.

Can my family come with me?

In most cases, yes. Spouses and dependent children can typically accompany a principal worker. Spouses of skilled workers and most international students are often eligible for open work permits, and children can attend school. We help you put the whole family on the right paperwork.

Which province should I choose?

The honest answer: the one where the right job, the right community and the right cost of living line up for your family. That’s a real conversation we have with every client — we don’t just chase whichever province processes fastest.

Do I need to speak English (or French)?

For most economic immigration programs, yes — you’ll need to demonstrate language ability through an approved test (IELTS, CELPIP or TEF). The minimum level depends on the program. We’ll tell you exactly what score you need to aim for.

Will my foreign credentials and experience be recognized?

For many trades and licensed professions, yes — but the process varies by province and trade. We help you understand what credential assessment (ECA) or certification steps you’ll need, ideally before you arrive.

What happens after I arrive in Canada?

We don’t disappear after the visa. We’ll help you with practical things like SIN, health card, banking and connecting you with settlement organizations in your region. Many of our clients later come back to sponsor family — and we’re here for that too.

Is Amicus licensed?

Yes. We’re authorized to represent clients under Canadian immigration regulations through the College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants (CICC). Always insist on a licensed representative — and never trust anyone who guarantees a visa.

Quick Checklists

What to gather before our first call

You don’t need all of this to start — but the more you have ready, the faster we can give you concrete advice.

Worker file checklist

  • Passport (and any old passports)
  • CV / resume with full work history
  • Educational certificates and transcripts
  • Language test results (if you have them)
  • Trade certifications or licenses
  • Reference letters from past employers
  • Marriage and birth certificates for family
  • Police clearance certificates (if available)

Employer file checklist (LMIA)

  • Business registration and incorporation documents
  • Recent T4 summary and payroll records
  • Two years of financial statements
  • Detailed job description and wage information
  • Records of recruitment efforts in Canada
  • Transition plan (for high-wage streams)
